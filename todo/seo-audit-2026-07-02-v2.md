## Executive Summary

The homepage remains the strongest asset on the site — strong conversion copy, a rich and
mostly-valid schema graph, excellent LCP engineering, full security headers, and honest
trust signaling (no fake reviews). If the site were only the homepage, it would score in
the low 70s.

The blog actively damages the site. Sixteen posts were bulk-published in one ~7.5-hour
window on 2025-11-24 from a WordPress import (`dailycraft.techpersonastudio.com`) and
shipped without review. One post contains live WordPress demo/test content (a "toggle me"
accordion, a corgi test photo, dead jQuery demo code). Five-plus posts leak literal
AI-generation scaffolding — `SEO Keywords: ...` prefixes and `[insert image: ...]`
placeholders — into meta descriptions, OG tags, and visible headings. Two posts render
**10 `<h1>` tags each**. Zero posts have Article/BlogPosting schema. And 10 of 16 posts
target developers/creators (Next.js Server Actions, n8n, AI video workflows), not the
plumbers, salons, restaurants, and realtors the homepage sells to.

**The single most damaging fact of this audit:** the site sells "I'll fix your broken
website" while its own blog contains exactly the kind of broken, templated content the
homepage promises to fix. Any prospect who clicks "Blog" before submitting the audit form
can lose trust at the moment it matters most.

The off-site ceiling from v1 still stands and is unchanged: zero reviews anywhere, GBP not
verifiably public, zero Tier-1 citations (no Yelp/Clutch/BBB), `sameAs` corroboration
still thin, and no location/service landing pages despite schema targeting Worcester + Boston.

## Would a client sign? (requested verdict)

**Short answer: I'd request the free audit, but I would not sign a paid build today.**

**What would pull me in.** The homepage is genuinely persuasive — the "lose the customer
before anyone talks to them" framing is specific and true for service businesses; the
21× lead-response stat is sourced; the 3-step process with two free steps is low-risk;
"one person, start to finish, you reach me directly" is a real differentiator against
agencies; and the honesty ("Reviews in progress", samples labeled as samples) reads as
integrity, not weakness. The free audit ask is well-calibrated — low commitment, clear
deliverable, 2-day turnaround.

**What would stop me from paying.**
1. **No verifiable proof of work.** The only case study (Quán Phở) is "going live soon."
   The before/after is claims, not a clickable URL. A realtor comparing 3 agencies — the
   persona that checks reviews first — scores this site 3/10; competitors like Turek Web
   Design show 82 five-star reviews.
2. **The blog contradicts the pitch.** If I click Blog and see "toggle me / Hello There
   How Are You!", a corgi stock photo, or a heading that literally reads
   `[insert image: small business storefront with map pins...]` — from someone selling
   website quality — I close the tab. This is the highest-leverage trust fix on the site.
3. **No price anchor.** "Scoped before you commit" appears three times but there is no
   "projects start at $X." A price-sensitive plumber assumes the worst and never calls.
4. **Search invisibility.** Searching "TechPersona Studio" surfaces only the site itself —
   no GBP panel, no Yelp, no Clutch, no third-party corroboration that this business exists.

**What flips the verdict to yes:** Quán Phở live + linked, 2–3 real Google reviews, a
"projects start at $X" anchor, and either fixing or unpublishing the blog. With those
four done, this site would outperform most local competitors on copy alone.
